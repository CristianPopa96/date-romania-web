# What Date România is

## The problem

Romanian public money is published, but in pieces that do not fit together. Follow one
leu from the budget to the company that finally gets it, and this is what you find:

| Step of the money                             | Is it published?                                  | Can it be tied to the next step?                    |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| How much an institution gets in its budget    | Yes                                               | Only through budget codes                           |
| What the institution commits to spend         | Only totals                                       | No company or contract is named                     |
| Which company wins a contract (SEAP)          | Yes                                               | The contract does not say which budget line pays it |
| What is actually paid to the company          | Mostly no; a few institutions publish voluntarily | Usually no contract reference                       |
| Who the company pays further (subcontractors) | No                                                |                                                     |

This is not anyone hiding things on purpose. Payments go through the State Treasury,
which publishes only totals; the budget, contract and payment systems have no common
number to join them; and the law on public information (Law 544/2001) gives you the
documents when you ask, not by default.

So nobody can show the whole road of a leu from published data alone. Date România does
the next best thing.

## What it does

1. **Shows both ends of the money.** What an institution has and spends on one side,
   which companies win its contracts on the other, tied together wherever the data
   allows.
2. **Puts everything about a place in one page.** Type the name of a commune and see its
   town hall's budget, the national programme money it received, the contracts signed
   there and the companies that won them.
3. **Ranks and spots patterns.** The largest contracts, the companies that win the most,
   the institutions that spend the most, and repeated purchases just under the legal
   limit.
4. **Marks the gaps.** Where something is not public, the page says so and why, instead
   of leaving a blank or a zero. The plan is to request the missing documents under Law
   544/2001 and publish what comes back.

## Who it is for

| Visitor                 | Typical question                                                  | Where they start                     |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| A citizen               | "What did my town hall spend, and on whom?"                       | Search, or the "Localități" section  |
| A journalist or NGO     | "Who are the biggest winners of this buyer? Is anything unusual?" | The rankings, a company page         |
| A local councillor      | "Where did the Anghel Saligny money for our commune go?"          | The programme page, or the commune   |
| Someone who reuses data | "Can I get this as a spreadsheet?"                                | The sources page, the export buttons |

[What you can find out](What-You-Can-Find-Out) goes through these questions one by one.

## What makes it strong

- **Every number shows its source.** Under each figure is a line such as "Sursa: SEAP ·
  achiziții directe · preluat 10.10.2026" (source, kind of record, day it was read). For
  one purchase or contract, that line opens a panel with a link to the original page and
  a digital fingerprint of the copy kept, so anyone can check it.
- **Nothing is thrown away.** Everything received from a public source is kept exactly
  as it came, before it is read. Any figure can be traced back, and any reading can be
  redone if a mistake is found.
- **Honest counting.** Only money really committed is added up: a refused purchase is
  listed but not counted, a spending ceiling is never counted as spending, a contract
  won by several companies together is never split, and figures covering different
  periods are never added to each other. See [Reading the figures](Reading-the-Figures).
- **Links say how sure they are.** When two records are tied together, the site says
  whether the link is sure, probable or possible, and on what evidence.
- **Gaps are shown, not hidden.** "Nepublicat" means the source does not publish the
  value; "Încă necolectat" means it does and Date România has not collected it yet.
  Neither is ever shown as a zero.
- **Facts, not accusations.** A pattern is labelled "Tipar observat, nu o acuzație" (an
  observed pattern, not an accusation). Colours never pass judgement on an institution
  or a company.
- **One programme followed end to end.** The Anghel Saligny payment files name the
  company that builds each project, so the site follows the money from the ministry to
  the town hall, the project, the builder and, probably, the contract.
- **Easy to share and reuse.** Every page and view has its own link. Lists download as
  spreadsheets (CSV, opens in Excel). The data is free to reuse, naming the source.
- **Readers can report errors.** Every page has "Raportează o eroare". A person checks
  each report, and accepted corrections are published.
- **Personal data is protected.** A sole trader can appear in SEAP under their personal
  ID number (CNP); it is hidden everywhere on the site.

## What it does not do yet

- **Budgets** are shown only for town halls and county councils, for 2024. The budgets
  of schools, hospitals, ministries and other institutions are published by the Finance
  Ministry behind a verification code that blocks automatic reading; a request for the
  whole set is planned.
- **Contracts** cover only 2026, from 1 January; earlier years are not collected.
- **Payments**: it does not know whom a town hall paid, outside the Anghel Saligny
  programme, because those payments are mostly not published.
- **EU-funded projects** are held for the PNRR and the national cohesion programmes,
  not yet for the regional programmes of 2021–2027.
- **Politicians and parties** come later.
